Consultations in AYUSH settings still start from a blank page.

The Ministry of Ayush and the All India Institute of Ayurveda asked for patient case-taking software that respects both classical history-taking and modern records. Doctors lose time reconstructing stories; patients repeat themselves; paper files go missing; emergencies are easy to miss in a busy OPD.

The problem

Unstructured intake. Mixed languages. Handwritten prescriptions. No single timeline. No first-class prakriti / nidana capture. No deterministic safety net.

The approach: pre-clinical case-taking

The patient tells their story in their own language — voice, text or documents. The AI structures it into a clinical context with provenance. The doctor reviews and verifies. The AI never sits in the diagnosis chair.

The flow

Patient → AI (structures, never invents) → structured context (versioned, source-labelled) → Doctor (reviews, corrects, verifies). Every transition is a database state change, not a screen animation.

What we refuse to fake

Missing information stays “not provided” — it never becomes “no history”. Skipped fields are stored as unknown, not guessed. Two visits are “related” only on structured data (pathway, medications, allergies) — never plain-text similarity. No button exists that doesn’t do something real.

Who it is for

OPD patients who speak different Indian languages, Kayachikitsa and Panchakarma clinicians, and institute administrators who must see every saved history and every urgent alert.

Where this build sits

A complete working prototype: real roles, records, workflows, audit and safety rules. The AI, OCR and speech services run on deterministic demo providers behind standard interfaces — the point is the clinical workflow, not model quality. See the Roadmap for what comes next.
